What's inside

Everything you use. Nothing you don't.

Drift runs on Chromium, the engine inside Chrome and Edge, so sites work like they should. Everything around the page is Drift's own: calm, quick, and out of your way.

Tabs down the side

Long titles stay readable. Drag to reorder, collapse with Ctrl B, and flip between recent tabs with Ctrl Tab, just like Alt Tab.

Ads blocked, built in

Uses the same filter lists as uBlock Origin: EasyList, EasyPrivacy, annoyances and malware lists. No extension to install.

One box for everything

Ctrl L searches the web, your history and your open tabs at once. Your home page and search engine are yours to pick.

Bookmarks, and bring yours

Star a page with Ctrl D. Import your bookmarks and history from Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Brave, Opera or Vivaldi in one click.

Chrome extensions

Add extensions from the Chrome Web Store or Edge Add-ons. Pin them to the toolbar and open their pop-ups like in Chrome.

Polite prompts

When a site asks for your camera, location or notifications, a slim bar slides in above the page. Sharing your screen? Pick exactly what it sees.

Downloads you can see

A progress ring in the toolbar and a clear list with pause, resume and "show in folder". Ctrl J opens it.

Updates itself

New versions download quietly and install the next time you close Drift. Your tabs come back exactly where you left them.

Open source

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT license. Read it, build it yourself, or help make it better.

Privacy

Your browsing stays on your computer.

Drift has no account, no sign-in and no analytics. There's no Drift server that could collect anything.

Drift is open source, so you don't have to take our word for it: read the code. The Chromium engine inside Drift downloads spell-check dictionaries from Google the first time you type in a text box.

  • History, tabs and settings are localThey're saved in %AppData%\Drift on Windows and ~/.config/Drift on Linux, and never uploaded. Clear them from History, or delete the folder.
  • No tracking, no telemetryDrift doesn't count you, profile you or phone home about what you do.
  • Only two things go online on their ownDrift asks GitHub whether a new version is out (a few times a day), and downloads the ad-blocking lists every 4 days. Neither request includes anything about you or your browsing. Extensions are downloaded only when you add one.
  • Trackers blocked by defaultEasyPrivacy and uBlock's privacy lists stop most tracking scripts before they load.
  • Sites ask before they get anythingCamera, microphone, location and notifications are off until you allow them, and you can take that back in Settings → Site permissions.
Install

Up and running in a minute.

No administrator rights needed on Windows. Drift installs just for you, and updates itself from then on.

Download DriftSetup.exeFrom the button at the top of this page (about 160 MB: Drift brings its own engine).
Open itIf Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", choose More info → Run anyway. That happens for new apps that aren't signed with a paid certificate yet.
Click InstallDrift goes in your Start menu (and on your desktop if you like). Uninstall any time from Settings → Apps.

FAQ

Questions

Is Drift free?

Yes. No ads, no paid tier, no account.

Will websites work?

Yes. Drift is built on Chromium, the engine Chrome and Edge are built on, so modern sites, video and web apps work normally. If the ad blocker breaks a site, click the shield and turn it off for that site.

How do updates work?

Drift checks this project's GitHub releases when it starts and every few hours. A new version downloads in the background, is checked against the SHA-256 checksum GitHub publishes, and installs when you close Drift, or right away if you click "Update" in the toolbar. On Linux the AppImage updates itself the same way; if you installed the .deb, Drift tells you when a new version is out and you run the install command again.

What's different on Android?

It's the same Drift, made for one hand: the address bar sits at the bottom, tabs open in a grid, and the menu slides up. The ad blocker uses the same filter lists as on your computer. Android's built-in web engine doesn't run extensions or show website notifications, so those aren't there.

Why is Drift 2 bigger than Drift 1?

Drift 1 borrowed Windows' built-in Edge WebView2 engine, which kept it tiny but tied it to Windows. Drift 2 carries its own copy of Chromium. That's what lets it run on Linux, and Drift now controls its own engine version.

Can I use Drift without installing it?

Yes. Download the portable .zip, unzip it anywhere and open Drift.exe. It updates itself in place, too.

Do Chrome extensions work?

Many do. Open an extension on the Chrome Web Store or Edge Add-ons in Drift and click "Add to Drift" in the bar above the page. Drift shows what the extension can do before adding it. Simple extensions, like dark modes and page tweaks, work best; ones that need newer Chrome-only features, like capturing a tab's sound, may not.

Can I bring my bookmarks and history?

Yes. Go to Settings → Your data → Import bookmarks and history, and pick Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Brave, Opera or Vivaldi. It works even while that browser is open, and doesn't change it. Passwords can't be imported, so you'll sign in to sites once in Drift.

Is the source code available?

Yes. Drift is open source under the MIT license: github.com/lompendans/drift. The README explains how to build it yourself.

How do I uninstall it?

On Windows: Settings → Apps → Installed apps → Drift → Uninstall. You can choose to keep or delete your history and settings. On Linux: run the install command with --uninstall at the end (see Install above), or remove the drift package.

Give your tabs some room.

Drift is free, open source, and installs in a few seconds.
